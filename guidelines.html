STUDENTS / SUPERVISION / MENTORING

Guidelines

A good advising relationship is built by both sides. This page explains what I expect from the undergraduate, master’s and Ph.D. students I supervise at the Institute of Computing (IC/UFF) and in the UFFeScience Research Group, and what you can expect from me in return. If you are thinking about working with me, please read it before we talk.

STUDENTS / RESPONSIBILITIES

What I expect from you

Meetings and communication

  1. 1

    Meet every two weeks

    We meet regularly, every 15 days. These meetings are the backbone of your work: they keep the research moving and help us catch problems early.

  2. 2

    Be on time, and cancel in advance

    Please arrive on time. If you cannot attend, let me know as early as possible so we can reschedule.

  3. 3

    Come with an agenda

    Bring a short agenda to every meeting: what you did, what you found, where you are stuck and what you plan next. Preparing it will help you organize your own ideas.

  4. 4

    Keep me updated

    Keep me informed about the progress of your work, including between meetings. Good news and bad news are both useful; surprises close to a deadline are not.

  5. 5

    When in doubt, ask

    There are no silly questions. Asking early saves days of work going in the wrong direction.

Planning and deadlines

  1. 6

    Know your program’s deadlines and rules

    Stay on top of the deadlines and regulations of your program: course credits, qualifying exams, proficiency tests, scholarship reports and defense dates are your responsibility.

  2. 7

    Don’t leave things to the last minute

    Plan ahead. Papers, reports and theses need time for review and revision, by you, by me and by your co-authors.

Autonomy and guidance

  1. 8

    Ask for help, and explore on your own

    Come to me when you are having difficulties, but don’t stop there: look for alternatives independently, read, test ideas and bring what you tried. Building this autonomy is a central goal of graduate training.

  2. 9

    Follow the guidance you receive

    Follow the directions and suggestions given in our meetings. If you disagree with one, tell me and explain why; a good argument is always welcome.

Research practice

  1. 10

    Act with research integrity

    Report results honestly, cite the work you build on and never copy text, data or code without proper credit. Discuss authorship and the use of AI tools openly with me and your co-authors.

  2. 11

    Make your work reproducible

    Keep code, data and experiment scripts versioned and documented, preferably in the group’s repositories, so that others, including your future self, can rerun and extend what you did.

  3. 12

    Be part of the group

    Take part in group meetings and seminars, share what you learn and help colleagues when you can. Treat everyone in the group with respect.

ADVISOR / COMMITMENTS

What you can expect from me

  1. A

    Regular meetings

    I keep our meeting schedule and give you advance notice if I need to reschedule.

  2. B

    Honest, constructive feedback

    I read your drafts and results and tell you clearly what works and what needs to improve.

  3. C

    A feasible research direction

    I help you define a topic that is relevant, doable within your program’s timeline and of interest to both of us.

  4. D

    Support for your growth

    I support you in publishing, presenting at conferences, building collaborations and planning your next career step.

COMMUNICATION / WELL-BEING

When things get difficult

Research has hard moments: experiments fail, papers get rejected and personal life gets in the way. If something is affecting your work, talk to me early. We can almost always find a way to adjust the plan when we know about a problem in time.
